The [LinkedIn content workflow](/playbooks/linkedin-content-workflow) covers how to produce one good post. This page covers how to produce them continuously, across more than one surface, without it becoming the only thing you do.

The distinction matters because content is the motion founders most often abandon. Not because it stops working — because there's no forcing function, and a week of silence costs nothing visible on the day it happens.

## What content is actually for

Content is not a lead source you can budget against. It's the thing that makes your other motions cheaper.

- **Outbound gets warmer.** A connection request from someone whose posts a buyer has already seen is a different message than one from a stranger.
- **Paid gets cheaper.** People who recognize the name convert at a better rate on the same ad.
- **Sales calls get shorter.** Prospects who read your writing arrive already agreeing with the premise, so the call is about fit rather than education.

Which is why the [pipeline system](/playbooks/pipeline-system) puts content in Stage 1 alongside founder-led outbound rather than treating it as a separate program. Judged on its own attribution it will always look mediocre; judged on what it does to everything else it's usually the highest-leverage thing you run.

## Pick one primary surface

Running three channels badly beats nothing, but not by much, and it costs three times as long. Choose where your buyers actually are and put everything else downstream of it.

| Surface | Choose it when | Realistic cadence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **LinkedIn** | B2B, and your buyers are reachable there — true for most B2B software | 2–3 posts a week |
| **X** | Developer, technical, or founder audiences | Higher frequency, shorter form |
| **Newsletter** | You have something long-form to say and a list to say it to | Weekly or biweekly |
| **Blog / SEO** | The category has real search volume and you can commit for months | 2–4 substantial pieces a month |

For most B2B founders the answer is LinkedIn primary, everything else derived from it. SEO is worth starting early precisely because it's slow — but it should never be the surface you start with, since it gives you no feedback for months.

## The weekly engine

The workflow that survives contact with a real week has three parts, and only one of them is writing.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Capture, continuously">
    The scarce input is not writing time — it's material. Screenshot posts you react to, save objections from sales calls, note the sentence a customer used that landed better than yours.

    A running backlog is what separates a sustainable cadence from a weekly panic. Most of your best posts are a sharp response to something specific, and you can't manufacture "something specific" on a Tuesday morning.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Draft in one sitting">
    Batch it. One session, two or three posts, using the [LinkedIn content workflow](/playbooks/linkedin-content-workflow) — source, brain-dump, let Emma codify, then edit.

    Batching works because the expensive part is loading context, not writing. Doing three at once costs far less than three times one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Repurpose deliberately">
    One idea, several surfaces. Not copy-paste — each surface wants a different shape.

    | From | To | How |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | LinkedIn post | X thread | Break the argument into beats; sharper, less throat-clearing |
    | LinkedIn post | Carousel | 6–8 slides, one claim per slide |
    | Three related posts | Newsletter issue | The connective tissue between them is the new value |
    | Newsletter issue | Blog post | Add the evidence you cut for length; this is your SEO surface |
    | Any of the above | Ad copy | A hook that already earned attention organically is a tested hook |

    That last row is the most underused. Your best-performing organic opening line is the closest thing to pre-validated ad creative you will get for free.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the backlog on Friday">
    What's drafted, what's scheduled, what went stale. Ten minutes, per the [operating rhythm](/playbooks/operating-rhythm).
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Feeding the loop back

Content is the one motion that produces market research as a byproduct. Use it.

- **Comments are free ICP research.** Who engages, what they push back on, which claim gets quoted. This belongs in Market Strategy.
- **Your best hooks become ad creative.** Pull the openings from your top organic posts before writing ad copy from scratch.
- **Engagement is a lead source.** People who consistently react to your posts are warm. Ask Emma to pull post engagement and add recurring ICP-fit engagers to a CRM lead list.
- **Objections become content.** The thing you had to explain three times on calls last month is your next post.

## Tagging so it counts

Every link you put in a post needs UTM parameters, or the traffic lands in `direct` and content looks like it does nothing. Use the convention in [measurement](/playbooks/measurement):

```text
?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=organic-content&utm_campaign=<post-topic-slug>
```

Ask Emma to add them: _"Add UTM parameters for LinkedIn organic content to every link in this post."_

## What to watch

| Watch | Not |
| --- | --- |
| Profile visits and inbound DMs from ICP accounts | Impressions |
| Which specific posts preceded inbound conversations | Follower count |
| Comment quality — are buyers arguing, or is it peers congratulating you? | Likes |
| Whether outbound acceptance rates are climbing | Post-level virality |

That last one is the real signal, and it's indirect by nature. If content is working, founder-led outbound gets easier — that shows up in acceptance and reply rates before it ever shows up in attributed leads.

## Anti-patterns

- **Asking for post ideas with no source material.** Emma will produce ten. They will sound like nobody and perform accordingly. The brain-dump is the whole point.
- **Posting the first draft.** There's always one line that gives it away.
- **Scheduling publication unattended.** Draft on a schedule; publish with your eyes on it. Publishing never reaches full autonomy — see the [autonomy ladder](/playbooks/operating-rhythm#the-autonomy-ladder).
- **Abandoning after four weeks.** Content compounds on a horizon of months. Four weeks of posting tells you nothing except whether you can sustain four weeks of posting.
- **Chasing reach over relevance.** A post that reaches 50,000 people who will never buy is worse than one that reaches 500 buyers, because it teaches the algorithm to show you to the wrong audience next time.
