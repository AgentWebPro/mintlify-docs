AgentWeb has a small vocabulary that does a lot of work. Getting these five straight — **skill, work plan, scheduled chat, template, conversation** — removes most of the confusion people hit in their first month.

## The one-line version

| Term | One sentence |
| --- | --- |
| **Emma** | The AI marketing agent. Everything else on this page is something Emma uses or you configure for her. |
| **Agent Mode** | The chat interface in the Customer Portal where you work with Emma. Most work starts here. |
| **Conversation** | A single persistent chat thread. Context and installed skills live here and do not carry to the next chat. |
| **Skill** | A capability: a bundle of tool grants plus standing rules that change how Emma behaves while it's installed. |
| **Work plan** | A sequence: ordered steps with instructions, state, and checkpoints where you review before Emma continues. |
| **Scheduled chat** | A prompt Emma re-runs on a cadence, with a name, a schedule, and somewhere to send the result. |
| **Template** | A reusable HTML layout Emma fills in — structure and styling for email and recurring content formats. |
| **Brand Hub** | Where your brand identity, positioning, templates, and reference docs live. Emma reads it on every run. |
| **Knowledge Base** | The reference material inside Brand Hub that Emma searches and reads during conversations. |
| **Market Strategy** | Your business overview, value proposition, ICP segments, and competitors. |
| **ICP persona** | A validated definition of who you sell to — behavior-based, not just demographic. |
| **Founder profile** | Your personal voice description inside Brand Guidelines. Without it, Emma writes in a neutral brand voice. |
| **Lead list** | A group of leads in the CRM with its own pipeline stages, custom fields, and point of contact. |
| **Enrichment** | Emma researching leads and filling in missing fields, with a 2-lead preview before the batch runs. |
| **ICP score** | A 0–100 score Emma assigns a lead by reading it against your Market Strategy ICPs. |
| **Connected account** | An authorized link to an outside platform. One platform can have several, each granting different capabilities. |
| **Organization** | A shared workspace — members, roles, and shared brand assets, leads, campaigns, and integrations. |
| **GEO** | Generative Engine Optimization: your visibility inside AI-generated answers, tracked alongside SEO. |

## Skill vs. work plan

This is the distinction people conflate most, and the two compose rather than compete.

|  | Skill | Work plan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | A capability — tools plus standing rules | A sequence — ordered steps with gates |
| Question it answers | _Can_ Emma do this? | _In what order_, with what checkpoints? |
| Grants tools | Yes | No — it installs skills that do |
| Has order and state | No | Yes |
| Persists across chats | No | No, but the plan definition persists |
| Where you create it | Say "make this a skill" in chat, or **Settings → Skills** | Say "make this as a workplan" in chat, or **Settings → Workplans** |
| Where you run it | Install it in a conversation | **New Chat → Select a workplan** |

Reach for a **skill** when the reusable thing is judgment — "here's how we evaluate quality, always apply this lens." Reach for a **work plan** when the reusable thing is a sequence with checkpoints — "do these five steps in this order, stop here for my approval."

They connect at the step level: a work plan step declares the skills it needs, and launching the plan auto-installs them. Mature capabilities often ship as both.

<Note>
  Neither one is a schedule. To run either on a cadence, create a **scheduled chat** whose prompt launches it.
</Note>

## Scheduled chat vs. work plan

A work plan answers _what steps, in what order_. A scheduled chat answers _when, and where do the results go_. They're orthogonal, and a scheduled chat is how a work plan gets a cadence.

A scheduled chat needs four things: a name, a cadence, the prompt to re-run, and where to send results.

<Warning>
  **Unattended runs don't pause for approval.** Scheduled runs proceed on a best-effort basis rather than stopping to ask. Before scheduling anything, check whether a step sends, posts, or spends — if so, restructure it to end at a draft.
</Warning>

## Template vs. work plan

A **template** is a layout — the structure and styling of an email or a recurring content format. A **work plan** is a workflow — the steps that produce the content.

If what you want to reuse is _how the output looks_, that's a template. If it's _the sequence of steps that got you there_, that's a work plan.

## Conversation vs. everything else

A conversation is the unit of context. It holds your objective, Emma's output, your corrections, and any skills installed during it.

What carries between conversations: your Brand Hub, Market Strategy, Knowledge Base, CRM, connected accounts, and your saved work plans and skills.

What does **not** carry: installed skills, mid-thread corrections, and pasted context. That's why converting a good thread into a work plan is worth doing while the thread is still fresh.

## Connections aren't one thing

"Connected" is not a single state, and this trips people up constantly:

- **Same platform, different capabilities.** LinkedIn has three separate connections — organic posting, Outreach, and Ads. Having one doesn't grant the others.
- **A skill existing doesn't mean an account is connected.** Emma has Meta and Google Ads skills whether or not you've linked those accounts.
- **Connected doesn't always mean fully authorized.** A connection can be active while missing the specific permission a request needs.

Ask Emma _"what accounts do I have connected?"_ before assuming a channel is live. See [Capabilities](/capabilities) for which connection each action requires.
